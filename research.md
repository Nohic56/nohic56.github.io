<h2>Research Overview</h2>

<p>
  My research sits at the intersection of <strong>quantitative proteomics, bioinformatics, and
  experimental cell biology</strong>. I develop computational approaches to analyse large-scale
  mass spectrometry datasets and connect them to mechanistic models of the cell cycle.
  The central question: how do cells time mitosis with such extraordinary precision?
</p>

<hr>

<h2>Postdoctoral Research</h2>

<div class="research-card">
  <h3>Temporal regulation of APC/C during mitosis</h3>
  <p>
    Working in the <a href="https://www.kamenzlab.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Kamenz Lab</a>
    at the University of Groningen, I study how the Anaphase-Promoting Complex/Cyclosome (APC/C)
    is regulated over time during mitosis using <em>Xenopus laevis</em> egg extracts as a model system.
    Progressive phosphorylation acts as a molecular timing mechanism that controls substrate
    degradation order — giving cells a way to sequence mitotic events precisely.
  </p>
  <div class="research-grid">
    <div class="research-grid-col">
      <p class="col-label">Methods</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Quantitative proteomics</li>
        <li>Native mass spectrometry</li>
        <li>Crosslinking MS</li>
        <li><em>Xenopus laevis</em> egg extracts</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div class="research-grid-col">
      <p class="col-label">Key findings</p>
      <ul>
        <li>Progressive phosphorylation drives mitotic timing</li>
        <li>APC/C substrate ordering is temporally encoded</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
    <div class="research-grid-col">
      <p class="col-label">Tools</p>
      <ul>
        <li>MaxQuant, Peaks, DIA-NN</li>
        <li>Custom Python / C++ scripts</li>
      </ul>
    </div>

<h2>PhD Research</h2>

<div class="research-card">
  <h3>Quantification of proteome variations at the intact protein level</h3>
  <p>
    During my PhD at the <a href="https://www.ijm.fr/?lang=en" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Institut Jacques Monod</a>
    (Camadro Lab, Université Paris Cité), I developed the <strong>bSLIM</strong> (bottom-up Simple
    Light Isotope Metabolic) labeling strategy — a novel quantitative proteomics workflow designed
    to improve the precision and depth of metabolic labeling experiments. The approach combines
    isotope labeling, advanced signal processing, and custom algorithms to measure proteome
    changes at scale.
  </p>
